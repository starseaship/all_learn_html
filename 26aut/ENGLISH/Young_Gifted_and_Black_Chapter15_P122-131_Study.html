克里斯汀的教育与理想、Sputnik 与冷战、学校种族隔离和布朗案、家庭支持与数学兴趣。

Reading · P122–131

P122

A changing future

Mary Jackson worked on research related to supersonic aircraft while high-school girls began to imagine wider career possibilities. Teaching remained a common career for women, but more choices were becoming possible.

玛丽·杰克逊参与超音速飞机研究的同时，女高中生开始想象更多职业可能性。教师仍是女性常见职业，但她们的选择正在增加。

P123

Christine follows the news

In October 1957, Christine Mann was a senior at the Allen School for girls in Asheville, North Carolina. As a student librarian, she arranged newspapers and magazines before other students arrived. She followed reports about nine Black students trying to attend Central High School in Little Rock. Federal troops were eventually sent to protect them.

1957年10月，克里斯汀·曼恩是北卡罗来纳州阿什维尔艾伦女子学校的毕业班学生。她每天很早到图书馆整理报刊，因此特别关注新闻。她读到九名黑人学生试图进入小石城中央高中，后来联邦军队被派去保护他们。

P124

A shocking headline

On October 4, 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik, the first artificial satellite. The news dominated American newspapers. At Langley, engineers faced pressure to improve American technology, while the public increasingly worried about the Soviet lead in space.

1957年10月4日，苏联发射世界第一颗人造卫星 Sputnik，迅速占据美国报纸头条。兰利的工程师面临提高美国技术水平的压力，公众也开始担心苏联在太空领域领先。

P125

The Sputnik Age

Sputnik made space travel feel real, but it also raised fears about Soviet missile technology and nuclear war. American leaders saw the launch as a challenge to the country's scientific and technological standing. During the Cold War, the space race became part of a larger struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union.

Sputnik 让太空旅行成为现实，也让美国人担心苏联的导弹技术与核战争。美国领导人把这一事件视为国家科技实力受到挑战的信号。冷战期间，太空竞赛成为美苏对抗的一部分。

P126

Who gets the chance to succeed?

Black newspapers questioned the contradiction between America's need for scientists and the barriers faced by Black students. Segregated education limited access to opportunity even while the country wanted scientific progress. Christine read these stories and felt both excitement about space and fear about nuclear weapons.

黑人报纸质疑美国的矛盾：国家需要科学人才，却仍限制黑人学生的教育机会。种族隔离阻碍人才成长。克里斯汀阅读这些新闻时，既对太空探索兴奋，也担心核武器。

P127

Living with nuclear fear

Public fallout-shelter signs and school duck-and-cover drills showed how deeply nuclear-war fears affected everyday life. The possibility of an attack was not merely an abstract political issue for Christine and other teenagers.

公共场所的防辐射避难所标志、学校的躲避演习，反映核战争的担忧如何进入日常生活。对克里斯汀等青少年来说，核袭击并非遥远的政治概念。

P128

Brown v. Board of Education

The chapter looks back to 1954, when Christine heard that the Supreme Court ruled against segregation in public schools in Brown v. Board of Education. Although this was a major legal victory, Christine worried about the educational gap between students from underfunded Black schools and those from better-resourced white schools.

这一章回顾1954年：克里斯汀得知最高法院在 Brown v. Board of Education 案中裁定公立学校种族隔离违宪。虽然这是重大法律胜利，她仍担心资源匮乏的黑人学校学生如何与白人学校学生竞争。

P129

Barbara Johns speaks up

Barbara Johns organized a student strike against poor conditions at Moton High School. The lawsuit that followed became connected to the broader legal challenge to school segregation. Christine realized that change could be both hopeful and frightening for students accustomed to unequal conditions.

芭芭拉·约翰斯组织学生罢课，抗议莫顿高中恶劣的学习条件。后续诉讼成为挑战学校种族隔离的一部分。克里斯汀意识到，对长期处于不平等条件下的学生而言，改革既带来希望，也带来不安。

P130

Family and education

Christine's parents, like the families of Dorothy Vaughan, Katherine Goble and Mary Jackson, valued education, honesty, hard work and character. They supported her schooling. An inspiring geometry teacher helped Christine see a future in mathematics, and she later considered Hampton Institute.

克里斯汀的父母和多萝西、凯瑟琳、玛丽的家庭一样，重视教育、诚实、努力与品格。他们支持她上学。一位富有感染力的几何老师激发了她的数学兴趣，她后来考虑进入汉普顿学院。

P131

A new beginning

Christine graduated from the Allen School in May 1958 as valedictorian. In June she left North Carolina to attend Hampton Institute. After seeing the river and the landscape near Hampton, she felt that new possibilities were opening in front of her.

克里斯汀于1958年5月以毕业生代表身份从艾伦学校毕业，并于6月离开北卡罗来纳州前往汉普顿学院。望着汉普顿附近的河流与景色，她感到人生出现了新的可能性。

种族隔离和不平等的教育机会如何影响克里斯汀·曼恩这样的黑人学生？

联系学校资源、课程机会，以及 Brown v. Board of Education 判决后克里斯汀的担忧。

可以从父母的支持和期待入手。
